# danluu.com

> danluu.com is Dan Luu's personal blog of long-form essays on software engineering, computer hardware, performance, latency, testing, hiring, and tech industry analysis. It is for programmers and technical readers looking for data-driven discussion of these topics.

- The homepage lists public posts hosted on danluu.com separately from Patreon posts, which are hosted on patreon.com rather than on the site itself.

## Guides

- [Some thoughts on writing](https://danluu.com/writing-non-advice/): Dan Luu's essay arguing writers should set style from their own goals rather than copy others, comparing Paul Graham, Julia Evans and others, plus his blogging process.
- [Branch prediction](https://danluu.com/branch-prediction/): A pseudo-transcript of a talk explaining CPU branch prediction, from pipelining costs through static and dynamic predictor schemes like two-bit, gshare, and hybrid.
- [What's new in CPUs since the 80s?](https://danluu.com/new-cpu-features/): A long technical essay answering a question about modern CPU features, covering 64-bit mode, caches, TLBs, out-of-order execution, memory ordering, and NUMA.
- [Some programming blogs to consider reading](https://danluu.com/programming-blogs/): A curated annotated list of programming blogs and writers, with commentary on what each covers and why a reader might find it useful.
- [Sampling v. tracing](https://danluu.com/perf-tracing/): A long essay arguing that sampling profilers miss tail-latency and rare performance bugs, illustrated with Google tracing examples, SHIM, and perf limits.
- [Malloc tutorial](https://danluu.com/malloc-tutorial/): A tutorial walking through writing a simple malloc implementation in C using sbrk, a linked list of block metadata, free, realloc, calloc, debugging with gdb, and exercises.
- [Latency mitigation strategies (by John Carmack)](https://danluu.com/latency-mitigation/): An archived copy of John Carmack's article on reducing VR head-tracking latency, covering sources of latency and host processing strategies.

## Blog

- [Futurist prediction methods and accuracy](https://danluu.com/futurist-predictions/): A long essay evaluating the accuracy of futurists' predictions, with summaries and critiques of Kurzweil, Fresco, Fuller and others.
- [Algorithms interviews: theory vs. practice](https://danluu.com/algorithms-interviews/): Essay arguing that algorithms interviews fail to predict on-the-job efficiency work, with examples of costly inefficiencies and a history of interview fads.
- [A defense of boring languages](https://danluu.com/boring-languages/): An essay arguing that boring programming languages like C, C++ and Java are underrated, with lists of major software written in each.

## Optional

- [There's no reason for software to be slow anymore](https://danluu.com/no-slow/): A blog post arguing that LLM coding agents make performance optimization cheap, with experiments on a regex engine, a game AI and ripgrep queries.
- [The benchmarkpocalypse](https://danluu.com/benchpocalypse/): A blog essay arguing that LLM agents make benchmark gaming easy, using a vibe-coded regex engine (FRE) as an example, with holdout test results and an appendix.
- [How does programming language affect token efficiency and correctness?](https://danluu.com/pl-tokens/): Dan Luu's post critiquing claims that dynamic or token-dense programming languages are more token efficient for LLMs, presenting his own zstd and Pandoc evals and an appendix on flaws.
- [Bad benchmarks and evals: Senior SWE-Bench, napkin math, and winter tires](https://danluu.com/exercise-7/): A blog post presenting benchmark-style exercises on napkin-math latency tables, AI coding evals (DeepSWE, Senior SWE-Bench), and winter tires, with critiques.
- [How web bloat impacts users with slow devices](https://danluu.com/slow-device/): Blog post arguing that web bloat makes sites unusable on slow devices, with a performance table comparing platforms on CPU and load times.
- [Diseconomies of scale in fraud, spam, support, and moderation](https://danluu.com/diseconomies-scale/): An essay arguing that large platforms have worse spam, fraud, moderation and support than smaller ones, citing examples from Meta, Google, YouTube and Stripe.
- [Seo Spam](https://danluu.com/seo-spam/): A long essay comparing search result quality across Google, Bing, Marginalia, Kagi, Mwmbl and ChatGPT, discussing SEO spam, ads and search engine incentives.
- [Why is it so hard to buy things that work well?](https://danluu.com/nothing-works/): An essay arguing that markets do not reliably select good products or efficient firms, citing examples of hiring, shipping, software vendors and in-house building.
- [Misidentifying talent](https://danluu.com/talent/): An essay on how scouts and hiring managers in baseball and tech judged talent by appearance, with examples of overlooked players and biased evaluations.
- [Cocktail party ideas](https://danluu.com/cocktail-ideas/): An essay arguing that people with superficial knowledge of a field often oversimplify its complexity, illustrated with civil engineering, bicycles, jam studies and computer architecture examples.
- [The container throttling problem](https://danluu.com/cgroup-throttling/): An internal-derived essay explaining how CFS quota throttling at Twitter causes tail latency and over-provisioning, with case studies and possible solutions.
- [Culture matters](https://danluu.com/culture/): An essay arguing that company culture drives engineering velocity, debugging skill and quality, alongside incentives and process, using examples from Centaur.
- [Some reasons to work on productivity and velocity](https://danluu.com/productivity-velocity/): An essay arguing that improving work velocity and productivity matters, rebutting common objections with personal examples and an appendix of online discussions.
- [The value of in-house expertise](https://danluu.com/in-house/): An essay arguing that companies at large scale benefit from in-house expertise such as kernel and JVM teams, rather than outsourcing it.
- [Measurement, benchmarking, and data analysis are underrated](https://danluu.com/why-benchmark/): Dan Luu argues that measurement and benchmarking of products and distributed systems have high impact, citing Jepsen, keyboard latency and car safety examples, and lists his measurement posts.
